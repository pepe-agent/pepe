# Release Notes 0.11.1

A release with one single subject: **the dashboard now works on mobile**. No new screens, no new features, the same pages as always, now readable at a width where they weren't before.

---

## 🐛 Fixes

### The dashboard is usable from a phone

The dashboard had been built for a wide window. The side menu took up 256 pixels and never left the screen at any width, so on a 390-pixel phone only 134 pixels were left for content: every section read one word per line, buttons ran off the edge, and the page scrolled sideways.

Now, below 768 pixels, the menu becomes a drawer that slides over the content. You open it with the three-line button next to each section's title, and close it by tapping outside it, pressing Escape, or just picking where to go. Along with that:

- Each section's action buttons move below the title instead of competing for space with it.
- Two-column forms become one column.
- Margins shrink, so a sixth of the screen isn't wasted on empty space at the sides.
- Buttons that only appeared on hover (pausing or deleting a conversation) are now always visible on touch, where there's no such thing as hovering.

At 768 pixels and up, nothing changed: the dashboard on a computer is exactly what it was.

📍 *Where to see it: any dashboard page, on a phone*

### Chat shows one screen at a time on mobile

The conversation list and the open conversation always sat side by side. On a phone that meant two cramped columns; on a tablet, a third column that left the conversation itself about 190 pixels wide.

Now the two only appear together at 1024 pixels and up. Below that, it's one screen at a time: the list, and once you pick a conversation, the full conversation with a back button in the corner.

📍 *Where to see it: dashboard → Chat*

### The message box no longer hides behind the browser bar

The dashboard measured height against the browser's full screen, without accounting for the phone's address bar appearing and disappearing as you scroll. The result: the message box, and the buttons at the end of a long form, ended up underneath it.

The measurement now tracks the actually visible area. The login screen, which had a fixed width, also stopped overflowing on narrow screens.

---

## 🔧 Under the hood

- The menu drawer is built with pure CSS: a hidden checkbox and labels that point at it. None of the dashboard's 19 sections needed its own code to get a working menu, and the drawer doesn't depend on the server responding to open.
- All 19 sections were checked at 360, 390, 768, 1024, and 1440 pixels wide, and none of them scroll sideways. The wide tables (usage, runs) still scroll within their own frame, as they already did.
